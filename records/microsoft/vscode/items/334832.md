# Issue #334832 — Local history folders are keyed by a 32-bit path hash with no collision handling: Timeline shows another file's entries and Restore overwrites with them

[https://github.com/microsoft/vscode/issues/334832](https://github.com/microsoft/vscode/issues/334832)

## Summary

The collision and cross-file content substitution remain directly traceable in current source, including a Restore path that overwrites the target file. Keep this open: both local history and hot-exit backups need resource isolation, not only a read-time guard.

## Change summary

Prevent colliding resource hashes from mixing local history, overwriting backups, or loading another file's content.

## Best solution

Ensure history and backups preserve separate content for distinct working-copy identities throughout writing, resolution, restoration, and deletion. Retain access to valid legacy data while establishing safe recovery behavior for legacy history folders whose entries may already be mixed.

- **Behavior:**

  > Files named 0b.txt and 1C.txt must retain independent local histories and unsaved backups despite their colliding legacy hashes. Timeline entries and Restore must never substitute another file's content. Opening or restoring a file must not load its neighbour's backup, and saving or deleting one resource's recovery data must not destroy the other's. Valid pre-existing history and backups must remain recoverable; treatment of already-mixed history requires a maintainer decision.
- **Trace:**

  > WorkingCopyHistoryModel.toHistoryEntriesFolder uses a 32-bit hash, and resolveEntriesFromDisk assigns every matching child to the requested resource without checking the listing's resource. WorkingCopyBackupServiceImpl.backup and resolve share the hash-derived location; resolve ignores the stored identity, while TextFileEditorModel.doResolve prefers that content over disk. The Restore command clones the selected history blob directly onto the attributed resource, establishing a concrete data-loss path. These paths remain present in the checkout, and the reporter's addendum at https://github.com/microsoft/vscode/issues/334832#issuecomment-5604168819 supplies the backup scenario. Keep the shared hash utility and unrelated consumers outside scope; this concerns persistent working-copy storage identity. The uncertainty is recovery of previously mixed history: its single listing resource cannot establish ownership of every blob.
- **Open decisions:**
  - When a legacy history folder may contain entries from multiple colliding resources, should ambiguous entries be excluded from normal Timeline/Restore while retained for explicit recovery, or remain available through another warned recovery experience?

## Evidence

- **Collision is deterministic:** src/vs/base/common/hash.ts:44-57 implements a signed 32-bit base-31 string hash. In the reported 0b.txt / 1C.txt pair, the adjacent character differences contribute 31 - 31 = 0; identical prefixes and suffixes preserve the collision.
- **History ownership is unchecked:** src/vs/workbench/services/workingCopy/common/workingCopyHistoryService.ts:118-120 derives the folder solely from hash(resource.toString()). resolveEntriesFromDisk():319-370 attributes matching folder children to the requested working copy without comparing entryListing.resource; readEntriesFile():527-540 only parses the listing.

Combined with the misattributed entries above, this copies the colliding file's history content onto the requested file.
