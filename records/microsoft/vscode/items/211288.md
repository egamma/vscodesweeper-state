# Issue #211288 — notebook progress accessibility signal refinement suggestions

[https://github.com/microsoft/vscode/issues/211288](https://github.com/microsoft/vscode/issues/211288)

## Summary

Keep open: current progress signals still use a generic, fixed-interval loop, leaving meaningful gaps in progress-aware cadence, background controls, and operation identification. This team-authored follow-up has neither a verified covering fix nor an older open canonical.

## Change summary

Refine notebook progress audio with determinate-progress feedback, adaptive repetition, optional background suppression, and clearer operation context.

## Best solution

Design progress feedback that distinguishes measurable completion from ongoing activity, reduces noise during long operations, and gives users meaningful control over background playback. Establish which progress information notebooks actually expose before promising percentage- or ETA-aware audio, and investigate wake/reconnection sounds separately rather than assuming their cause.

## Plan brief

- **Likely files:** `src/vs/workbench/contrib/accessibility/browser/accessibilityConfiguration.ts`, `src/vs/platform/accessibilitySignal/browser/progressAccessibilitySignalScheduler.ts`, `src/vs/platform/accessibilitySignal/browser/accessibilitySignalService.ts`, `src/vs/base/browser/ui/progressbar/progressAccessibilitySignal.ts`, `src/vs/base/browser/ui/progressbar/progressbar.ts`, `src/vs/workbench/contrib/notebook/browser/view/cellParts/cellProgressBar.ts`
- **Validation:** After agreeing the behavior, use controlled-timer tests for the chosen cadence and focus policy, including stopping playback after progress ends. Manually exercise a long-running notebook cell while focused, unfocused, collapsed, and completed; separately check whether a TQDM output exposes usable progress and identify any signal heard after wake/reconnection.
- **Behavior:**

  > Long-running notebook operations should provide useful nonvisual activity feedback without excessive repetition. Where reliable completion information exists, the experience should communicate progress according to an agreed policy rather than imply a percentage from elapsed time alone. Users should have the agreed control over feedback while VS Code is unfocused, and progress sounds should be attributable enough to avoid confusing unrelated activity with cell execution. Acceptance must cover known and unknown progress, foreground/background transitions, and stopping feedback when the operation ends.
- **Trace:**

  > CellProgressBar in src/vs/workbench/contrib/notebook/browser/view/cellParts/cellProgressBar.ts represents execution as infinite progress; it does not interpret TQDM output. ProgressBar.show() in src/vs/base/browser/ui/progressbar/progressbar.ts starts the scheduler through progressAccessibilitySignal.ts, and workbench.ts wires that factory to AccessibilityProgressSignalScheduler. The scheduler plays the generic progress signal at a default fixed interval, while accessibility.signals.progress exposes sound and announcement enablement only. The same scheduler is also used by chat, so notebook refinements must not silently redefine other consumers' behavior. The initial implementation discussion is https://github.com/microsoft/vscode/issues/196396; this follow-up preserves distinct refinements from https://github.com/microsoft/vscode/issues/196396#issuecomment-2073102309. Structured determinate-progress availability and the cause of wake/reconnection sounds remain unestablished.
- **Open decisions:**
  - Should determinate feedback cover only structured progress already supplied to VS Code, or also notebook-output progress such as TQDM?
  - What should users hear for determinate progress, and how should repetition respond to completion changes, elapsed time, or a reliable ETA?
  - Should background suppression apply only to progress feedback or to all accessibility sounds, and what should its default be?
  - What user-visible mechanism should identify the operation responsible for progress feedback?
