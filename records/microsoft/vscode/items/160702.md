## Plan brief

- **Likely files:** `src/vs/editor/browser/config/fontMeasurements.ts`, `src/vs/editor/browser/config/editorConfiguration.ts`, `src/vs/editor/browser/viewParts/viewLines/viewLineOptions.ts`
- **Validation:** On a current Chromebook or ChromeOS Flex system, open vscode.dev at 100%, 110%, and 125% display scaling, type at the end of a long monospace line, and verify that the caret, typed text, selections, and decorations remain aligned both before and after changing scaling and reloading; repeat with `editor.disableMonospaceOptimizations` enabled as the control.
- **Behavior:**

  > At fractional ChromeOS display scaling, the editor caret, typed characters, selections, and decorations must remain aligned at every column, including near the end of long lines. Changing the display scale and reloading must not introduce cumulative horizontal drift. The default optimized rendering path should match the alignment observed with `editor.disableMonospaceOptimizations` enabled.
- **Trace:**

  > Multiple ChromeOS reports were consolidated into https://github.com/microsoft/vscode/issues/160702, and disabling monospace optimizations consistently avoided the drift. ViewLineOptions in src/vs/editor/browser/viewParts/viewLines/viewLineOptions.ts enables that optimization when FontInfo reports a monospace font; FontMeasurementsImpl in src/vs/editor/browser/config/fontMeasurements.ts supplies the measured width, and EditorConfiguration in src/vs/editor/browser/config/editorConfiguration.ts recomputes options when PixelRatio changes. The uncertainty starts at whether ChromeOS reports unstable glyph metrics, whether cached measurements become inconsistent after a scale change, or whether optimized column multiplication diverges from browser layout only at fractional DPR. Do not conflate this text-caret defect with the native mouse-pointer scaling tracked by https://github.com/microsoft/vscode/issues/136390, and do not globally disable the optimization without measuring its performance impact.
- **Open decisions:**
  - Can the defect still be reproduced on current ChromeOS, and does it require changing scaling after font measurements have been cached?
  - Is the mismatch caused by stale font measurements, fractional-width rounding in optimized line positioning, or browser/ChromeOS glyph metrics?
  - Should any mitigation invalidate measurements on scaling changes, reject unreliable monospace measurements, or narrowly disable optimization for the affected environment?
