# Issue #74342 — [themes] Allow to set a background color for settings / webview editors

[https://github.com/microsoft/vscode/issues/74342](https://github.com/microsoft/vscode/issues/74342)

## Summary

The requested separation between code-editor and informational-editor colors remains missing in the inspected settings surface. Keep this accepted Backlog request open; existing control-specific colors and the empty-group background do not cover it, and no qualifying open canonical was found.

## Change summary

Allow themes to color Settings and informational/webview editors independently of code editors, including foreground colors where appropriate.

## Best solution

Provide a consistent theme-color contract for informational editor backgrounds without changing code-editor colors or existing defaults. Settle whether Settings, built-in rich editors, and extension webviews share that contract or expose separate overrides before implementation.

## Plan brief

- **Likely files:** `src/vs/workbench/contrib/preferences/common/settingsEditorColorRegistry.ts`, `src/vs/workbench/contrib/preferences/browser/settingsTree.ts`, `src/vs/workbench/contrib/preferences/browser/tocTree.ts`, `src/vs/workbench/contrib/preferences/browser/settingsEditor2.ts`, `src/vs/workbench/contrib/webview/browser/themeing.ts`, `src/vs/workbench/contrib/webview/browser/pre/index.html`, `src/vs/workbench/contrib/extensions/browser/extensionEditor.ts`
- **Validation:** With a theme using visibly different workbench and code-editor backgrounds, open Settings, an extension details editor, and a representative webview. Verify that the agreed informational-surface override changes its complete intended surface without altering code editors, remains consistent through focus and selection changes, and updates on theme changes; removing the override must restore current defaults. Check foreground contrast and high-contrast themes.
- **Behavior:**

  > Users should be able to retain their preferred code-editor colors while giving informational editors a separate theme background. Settings must not retain code-background patches in its settings list or table of contents when its informational background is customized. The agreed rich-editor and webview scope should behave consistently, with readable foregrounds and unchanged defaults when no override is supplied.
- **Trace:**

  > The clarified request is recorded at https://github.com/microsoft/vscode/issues/74342; the empty-group workaround was already rejected in its predecessor discussions. SettingsTree and TOCTree explicitly use editorBackground for their list backgrounds and related states, while settingsEditorColorRegistry.ts exposes only component-specific overrides. WebviewThemeDataProvider exports registered colors, and the webview preload supplies a transparent body with editor foreground, so webview coverage requires examining both host backgrounds and authored content. The trace establishes the Settings gap but does not establish a universal override for arbitrary webview content. Preserve code-editor colors, existing unconfigured appearance, and extension-authored styling contracts; the unresolved boundary is which informational surfaces share the new theme semantics.
- **Open decisions:**
  - Should Settings and other informational editors share one background token, or should Settings and rich/webview editors have independently customizable tokens?
  - Should the rich-editor contract cover only built-in informational editors, or also establish defaults for extension-provided webviews while preserving their explicit styling?
  - Should matching informational-editor foreground overrides be included, or should existing foreground tokens remain the contract?

- **Existing capability does not cover the request:** src/vs/workbench/contrib/preferences/common/settingsEditorColorRegistry.ts registers header, dropdown, checkbox, input, focused-row and hovered-row colors, but no overall Settings-editor background. Those controls cannot independently recolor the full Settings surface.

Exporting existing colors does not itself establish an independent informational-editor background contract, and webview-authored styling must be considered.
