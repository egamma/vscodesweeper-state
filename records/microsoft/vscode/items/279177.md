## Evidence

- **Historical observation:** https://github.com/microsoft/vscode/issues/279177 and https://github.com/microsoft/vscode/pull/279172 report increases of 17 ChildProcessMonitor objects and debounce-associated functions after 17 task runs on Ubuntu. The supplied measurements do not establish whether these objects survive a settling interval and garbage collection or identify their strong retaining roots.
- **Current monitor lifecycle:** src/vs/platform/terminal/node/childProcessMonitor.ts no longer uses the debounce decorator: handleInput and handleOutput use registered RunOnceSchedulers and reject scheduling after disposal. src/vs/base/common/async.ts:1208-1220 cancels a scheduler's timeout and clears its runner during disposal. An already-running listProcesses query can still retain its monitor until completion.
- **Generic debounce hypothesis:** src/vs/base/common/decorators.ts stores reducer output, not the decorated method's return value; fn.apply(this, args) is not assigned to resultKey. The reported monitor path supplies no async reducer, and an object-Promise cycle alone does not establish a garbage-collection leak without an external retaining root.
- **Linked PR is narrower than the report:** https://github.com/microsoft/vscode/pull/338526 is merged, but explicitly describes bounded retention after disposal rather than a demonstrated permanent Promise cycle. Its reported Windows measurements settled to zero retained monitors in both baseline and candidate; original Linux/macOS-specific reproduction remained unverified. This is not sufficient new evidence to overturn the prior review's refuted completed-close claim.
- **Existing source-level coverage:** src/vs/platform/terminal/test/node/childProcessMonitor.test.ts covers cancellation of pending input/output timers, prevention of scheduling after disposal, and coalescing 17 input/output iterations. These tests were inspected, not executed, and do not establish heap stabilization for the reporter's Ubuntu task workflow.
